Attending ICAA 2026, Berlin, Germany

Xuezhou Wang is attending the International Conference on Aluminum Alloys (ICAA 2026), held 13–17 September 2026 in Berlin, Germany, and gave an oral presentation on 14 September 2026 titled “Multiscale modelling of solute clustering kinetics under the influence of excess vacancies during natural ageing of Al-Mg-Si alloys”, as part of Session J.2: Quench Sensitivity and Vacancy-Controlled Aging in 6xxx/7xxx Alloys, Hörsaal D.

Authors: Wang, X. (Speaker)1,5; Li, C.1; Xu, Y.2; Zhao, D.3; Li, Y.4
1Norwegian University of Science and Technology, Trondheim (NO); 2SINTEF Industry, Trondheim (NO); 3Tianjin University (CN); 4Suzhou Lab (CN); 5King's College London, London (UK)

Solute clusters formed during natural ageing (NA) play a crucial role in the age hardening response during subsequent artificial ageing (AA) in Al-Mg-Si alloys. However, existing numerical models have not fully captured the solute clustering behavior during NA. In this work, a multiscale modelling framework is developed to simulate solute clustering kinetics under the influence of quenched-in excess vacancies. The framework integrates a mesoscale cluster dynamics model to simulate the evolution of cluster size distribution and a vacancy model to account for vacancy diffusion and annihilation at grain boundaries and dislocations. The formation energies of solute clusters and the vacancy trapping energies for clusters of different sizes are rigorously extracted from atomistic Monte Carlo simulations.

The enhanced solute diffusion arising from quenched-in excess vacancies, the gradual trapping of vacancies by growing solute clusters, and the strong suppression of vacancy diffusion due to cluster trapping are well addressed. The modelling framework is validated against experimental measurements of cluster number density and average cluster size during NA in a commercial 6060 alloy. It is further applied to investigate the influence of quenching rate on NA solute clustering kinetics, providing a mechanistic explanation for the experimentally observed non-proportional quenching effect on NA hardening kinetics in an industrial 6014 alloy.

The modelling results reveal the solute clustering mechanism and kinetics, in particular under the influences of excess vacancies during NA, which may deepen the understanding of NA behavior and its impact on the subsequent AA response.

In the same session, a companion study co-authored by Wang, “Unraveling the kinetics of solute clustering during natural ageing in Al-Mg-Si alloys by a machine learning aided kinetic Monte Carlo simulation”, was presented by Y. Li (13:00–13:15, Hörsaal D).

Authors: Wang, X.1,4; Li, C.1; Xu, Y.2; Li, Y. (Speaker)3
1Norwegian University of Science and Technology, Trondheim (NO); 2SINTEF Industry, Trondheim (NO); 3Suzhou Lab (CN); 4King's College London, London (UK)

Natural ageing (NA) has strong influences on the age hardening response during subsequent artificial ageing (AA) in commercial 6xxx aluminium alloys, as NA-induced solute clusters significantly affect the precipitation kinetics of β″ precipitates. However, the underlying mechanisms remain to be fully understood. In this study, a kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulation framework is developed to investigate solute clustering behavior during NA in 6xxx alloys. Central to this framework is a physics-informed interatomic bond model that determines the local configurational energy as a function of local lattice occupations. A machine learning genetic algorithm is applied to systematically parameterize the bond energies between different atoms and vacancies, based on the formation enthalpies of representative solute cluster and GP-zone structures identified in recent experimental studies. Vacancy migration barriers are calculated on the fly using an improved kinetic resolved activation method, which addresses the local lattice distortion effect to more accurately reflect realistic diffusion behavior.

The formation of representative single β″-eye type GP zones is successfully reproduced within the simulation supercell. The NA clustering process shows fast kinetics in the initial stage driven by a high concentration of free excess vacancies, followed by slow kinetics due to strong vacancy trapping by large clusters. Additionally, the influence of alloy composition on solute clustering behavior during NA is qualitatively examined. This study provides valuable insights into the interplay between solute clustering and vacancy trapping during NA in 6xxx alloys, thereby advancing the understanding of fundamental mechanisms governing how NA influences the subsequent AA response.
